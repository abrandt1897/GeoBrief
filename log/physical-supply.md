## East-West line after the Khurais strike (Oct 5, late) — status CONTESTED

**Bottom line:** leans **flowing, possibly at a reduced rate, after a brief weekend halt** (~75%). ~25% it is effectively down for days (would fire "East-West/Yanbu offline for days" → infra ~16%). Not confirmed: no tracking data and no Saudi statement since the strike.

**Confirmed (high confidence):**

- A real, large strike/fire at **Pumping Station No. 2 (Khurais, east of Riyadh)** on Oct 4: Sentinel-3 shows a ~50 km black smoke plume; NASA FIRMS logged 4 heat anomalies, FRP up to 88 MW, one pixel at the VIIRS 367 K ceiling (HormuzLetter / OSINT, via No1's Daily Digest). Houthis claimed it (Saree). Damage is not in doubt; "restored" ≠ "unharmed."
- The line **was halted at least briefly** over the weekend — AFP's source says so and Bloomberg acknowledges a brief weekend halt.
- **Pre-strike throughput was high:** Bloomberg Oct 2 (one unnamed person) — ~6 Mbd, >80% of 7 Mbd nameplate, ~4.5 Mbd left for export after west-coast refineries. The Kpler ~2.65 Mbd figure (≈Sep 30) is superseded.
- Saudi government and Aramco have said **nothing**.

**Why it leans "flowing":**

- Two independent wires vs one: **Bloomberg** ("people familiar") and **Reuters** (unnamed source, per CBS/Yahoo) say oil is flowing Monday; **AFP** (one unnamed Saudi energy-sector source) says "big damage… pipeline stopped again."
- **Market vote:** Brent rose up to 1.2% on the AFP report and pared the gain on Bloomberg's.
- **Aramco cut Nov Arab Light OSP to Asia** $3 to Oman/Dubai −$5, a six-year low (Reuters, Oct 5) — a seller bracing for a supply outage doesn't usually cut price to win volume. Weak signal: the OSP may have been set regardless of Khurais.
- Plausible reconciliation (inference, not reported): the system can likely run derated around one damaged pumping station, so AFP may describe the weekend halt and Bloomberg/Reuters Monday's restart.

**Why it is NOT confirmed:**

- Every flow claim on both sides is a single anonymous source; Yeni Şafak / Anadolu / Core Insights are rewrites, not independent confirmation.
- No Kpler / Vortexa / TankerTrackers reading on East-West throughput or Yanbu loadings after Oct 4.
- Throughput rate after restart is unknown — "flowing" could be well below the pre-strike ~6 Mbd.

**What resolves it:** Yanbu/Al Muajjiz loadings for Oct 4–8 (Kpler, Vortexa, TankerTrackers satellite counts), or an Aramco/Energy Ministry statement. Check on the next runs.

**Arab Light OSP (context):** Arab Light = Saudi flagship medium-sour grade (~33° API, ~2% S, mostly Ghawar). OSP = Aramco's monthly differential to a benchmark (Asia: Oman/Dubai average). Reuters rationale for the Nov cut: record freight (VLCC Gulf→China >$30/bbl, FOB pricing so buyer pays), market share after war disruptions, and delays/longer voyages for cargoes loaded at Sidi Kerir (Egypt, via SUMED). A discount to a war-elevated benchmark, not cheap oil. Minor for US pump prices.

**Uhud (3rd UAE-linked hit, Oct 2):** ~46k DWT products tanker, built 2003, Panama flag; registered owner Master Energy LLC, commercial/ISM manager Emirates Shipping LLC (UAE) (MagicPort). Regional product trader (Fujairah, Hamriya, Khor Fakkan, Sohar, Port Sudan) — **not** a Saudi shuttle or STS-loop VLCC, so tw_saudi_ship does not fire.

**Oct 6 update:** Iran International reports the East-West line delivered 5.8M bbl to Yanbu by Tuesday morning — a cumulative/single-source figure, not a rate; supports "flowing" but still no Kpler/Vortexa Yanbu loadings. Houthis claim a strike on the Rabigh refinery (fed by an East-West spur); unconfirmed.

## Hormuz flows fall back as tanker attacks peak (Oct 11)

Kpler's weekly Hormuz figure has dropped from the late-September high: crude ran about 10.3 Mbd in the seven days to Oct 3 (about 76% of prewar) and about 8.5 Mbd in the week to Oct 7 (about 40% below prewar, per CNBC). Middle East crude exports fell to 15.3 Mbd in the week to Oct 7, from 18.5 a week earlier, as attacks on tankers reached a wartime high (JMIC: at least 12 between Sept 28 and Oct 2). Kpler counted only seven commodity carriers through the strait on Oct 6, the fewest since late July, with the sharpest drop in ship-to-ship transfer traffic off the Gulf of Oman.

- Products are still scarce: about 1.3 Mbd, 11% of Hormuz flows against more than 20% prewar, which keeps diesel tight while crude recovers.
- East-West/Yanbu is still unplotted: the Saudi figure of 5.8M bbl delivered is cumulative, and no tracker has given a post-Khurais loading rate.
- The two Kpler weekly figures don't share a stated basis (crude vs crude and products); read them as direction, not a precise level.

## East-West pipeline back near 5.8 Mbd (Oct 13)

The chart now plots the East-West pipeline at about 5.8 Mbd from Oct 6. That is the Saudi energy minister's on-the-record figure ("as of this morning we're back up to 5.8 million barrels" a day, GCC forum in Manama), which he stood by at the WPC congress in Riyadh on Oct 12. It was earlier logged as a cumulative volume, which misread it. Kpler supports a quick recovery: Red Sea loadings came to about 45M bbl between Sept 17 and Oct 4 and rebounded faster than expected after the restart.

- This is an official estimate, not a tracker reading; no Kpler, Vortexa or TankerTrackers rate has been published since the Oct 4 Khurais strike.
- A Yanbu loadings figure (plus about 1 Mbd for west-coast refineries) would replace it.

## Hormuz exports stay near 8.5 Mbd as Iran sinks a container ship (Oct 14)

Kpler's media briefing puts Hormuz exports at 8.5 Mbd in the week to Oct 12, nearly 40% below prewar and level with the week to Oct 7, with daily flows swinging from 6 Mbd on Friday to 3 Mbd on Monday. Middle East crude shipments, including the pipelines to the Gulf of Oman and the Red Sea, slipped to 14.0 Mbd from 15.3. The IEA's October report confirms how far September had recovered: 10.9 Mbd of total oil through Hormuz (up 3.7 on August), Gulf crude exports of 13.9 Mbd and 5.6 Mbd of bypass exports. October is giving that back as attacks mount (UKMTO: 20 in the strait since Oct 1; GFS Pride sank Oct 14).

- Products remain the bottleneck: the IEA has Middle East product exports at 1.9 Mbd and LPG at 0.67 Mbd in September, against 4.7 and 1.5 prewar.
- East-West has no new reading; the plotted 5.8 Mbd is still the Saudi minister's Oct 6 figure.

## Hormuz crude edges up to 9.4 Mbd as Yanbu becomes a target (Oct 16)

Kpler puts crude exports through Hormuz at 9.4 Mbd in the week to Oct 14, about 70% of prewar and up from 8.5 in the week to Oct 12, even though at least nine ships were attacked in and around the strait over those days. The attacks are slowing traffic, not stopping it. On Oct 16 Saudi forces intercepted a Houthi ballistic missile aimed at the Yanbu export terminal, the first reported attempt this month on the Red Sea end of the East-West bypass.

- No hit was reported, so the East-West tripwire stays armed; a Yanbu outage of three days or more confirmed by a tracker would fire it.
- East-West still has no tracker reading; the plotted 5.8 Mbd is the Saudi minister's Oct 6 figure.
